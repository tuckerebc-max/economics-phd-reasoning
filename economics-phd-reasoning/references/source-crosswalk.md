## Source-grounded construct

**SOURCE:** QAA's Economics benchmark addresses theoretical and empirical understanding, analytical reasoning, mathematics / statistics, evidence, policy analysis, application, and communication. AEA describes doctoral preparation through advanced microeconomics, macroeconomics, quantitative methods / econometrics, fields, qualifying exams, research planning, and dissertation. Stanford Economics supplies a university-specific doctoral pathway.

## PhD boundary

**SOURCE:** The field-level sources describe degree expectations and common doctoral components; the Stanford program is one exemplar.  
**INFERENCE:** The textbook competency is to formulate and defend original economic research by constructing models, stating assumptions, identifying evidence, selecting credible methods, interpreting results, evaluating robustness, and communicating implications.  
**NOT CLAIMED:** One universal Economics PhD rubric or a single correct methodological tradition.

## Construct decomposition

1. Frame a substantive question and contribution.
2. Construct and interrogate a model with explicit assumptions.
3. Identify causal, descriptive, or structural claims and evidence needs.
4. Select quantitative, qualitative, experimental, or theoretical methods appropriately.
5. Analyze robustness, uncertainty, external validity, and alternative explanations.
6. Interpret policy or practical meaning without overclaiming.
7. Defend work in writing, seminar, peer review, and dissertation contexts.

## Evidence and task model

**CROSSWALK:** Model memo, assumptions audit, identification / methods plan, replication or robustness packet, empirical interpretation, literature-position memo, seminar defense, and dissertation proposal.  
**EVALUATOR DESIGN:** Score question-method fit, assumption transparency, evidence quality, identification / inference, robustness, interpretation, and response to critique.

## Textbook spine

`Question → Model → Identify evidence → Estimate / reason → Stress-test → Interpret → Defend → Revise`

## Failure modes and constraints

- model presented as reality;
- assumptions hidden;
- correlation treated as causal identification;
- robustness ritual without substantive interpretation;
- method chosen for availability rather than question;
- field or subfield diversity flattened;
- agent-generated equation or code accepted without derivation and checking.
